Cyber Security

Learn the principles of securing networks, data, and software systems against unauthorized digital intrusions, malicious attacks, and data breaches.

Core Concept

What is Cyber Security?

Cyber Security is the practice of protecting computers, servers, mobile devices, electronic systems, networks, and sensitive data from malicious attacks, digital theft, and unauthorized disruption.

It encompasses a combination of technical tools (firewalls, encryption, antivirus), security policies, and human awareness to maintain the confidentiality, integrity, and availability (the CIA triad) of digital information.

Why Cyber Security is Important

As society conducts more of its essential activities online—from banking and health records to university grading portals:

  • Protects Personal Privacy: Prevents unauthorized exposure of student credentials, identity numbers, and private chats.
  • Prevents Financial Loss: Safeguards against credit card fraud, ransomware extortion, and account takeovers.
  • Maintains Business Continuity: Ensures that vital web services and infrastructure remain operational 24/7.
Threat Landscape

Common Cyber Threats Explained

The most prevalent digital attack vectors encountered on the internet today.

🎣

Phishing

Social engineering attacks where attackers impersonate trustworthy institutions (banks, colleges, or service providers) through fake emails or websites to trick victims into revealing passwords or OTPs.

🦠

Malware

Any malicious software created to cause damage or gain unauthorized access. Common forms include viruses, trojans, spyware, keyloggers, and ransomware that encrypts user files for extortion.

🔑

Password Attacks

Techniques such as brute-force attacks, dictionary attacks, and credential stuffing where automated scripts try thousands of common passwords or previously leaked credential lists to crack accounts.

🔐 Data Protection & Encryption

Encryption scrambles readable plaintext into unreadable ciphertext using mathematical keys. Even if intercepted on a public network, encrypted data (such as HTTPS or TLS) cannot be read by eavesdroppers without the matching decryption key.

🌐 Safe Browsing Habits

Safe browsing means checking the URL padlock icon in the browser address bar, verifying that domain spellings match the legitimate service (avoiding typosquatting), and never downloading executable files from unverified third-party sources.

Student Practical Guide

Stay Safe Online: Practical Checklist

Actionable daily cyber hygiene practices that every student and developer should follow.

✓

Use Strong Passwords

Create passwords with at least 12 characters, mixing uppercase letters, lowercase letters, numbers, and symbols. Never use your birthdate or name.

✓

Enable Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)

Add a secondary verification step via an authenticator app (like Google Authenticator) for your email, college portals, and social media.

✓

Avoid Suspicious Links

Never click on links or download unexpected attachments from unknown senders claiming you won prizes or need urgent account verification.

✓

Keep Software Updated

Regularly update your operating system, web browsers, and apps to patch critical security vulnerabilities discovered by vendors.

✓

Do Not Share Passwords

Never share your passwords with friends, colleagues, or write them down on unsecured sticky notes attached to your computer monitor.

✓

Use Trusted Websites Only

Ensure websites use valid HTTPS SSL certificates before entering login credentials, bank details, or submitting college applications.

Career Specializations

Cyber Security Career Paths

Security Analyst

Monitors organization networks for security events, analyzes intrusion alerts, and remediates vulnerabilities.

Ethical Hacker / Pen Tester

Simulates real attacks with written authorization to identify defensive loopholes in software before criminals can exploit them.

SOC Analyst

Works in a 24/7 Security Operations Center triaging active alarms, tracing incident root causes, and executing containment protocols.
